# Jeclazon Information Architecture & Communication Authority

**Status:** Architecture

## Communication goal

Jeclazon communicates as a technology company: confident without exaggeration, technically literate without unnecessary jargon, concise without becoming vague, and ambitious without presenting future plans as current facts.

## Voice

Jeclazon copy should be:
- clear
- precise
- composed
- intelligent
- economical
- evidence-led
- globally understandable

Avoid:
- “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” “best-in-class,” “industry-leading,” or similar unsupported superlatives
- generic AI-generated corporate filler
- invented scale
- long mission prose before explaining what the company actually does
- excessive “we believe” language
- fake urgency
- repeated CTAs
- claims whose truth depends on future implementation

## Message hierarchy

Every major public page should answer, in order:

1. **What is this?**
2. **Why does it matter?**
3. **What exists today?**
4. **How does it work or what is distinctive?**
5. **Where can the visitor go deeper?**

Do not force all five answers into the hero.

## Homepage architecture

The homepage should evolve according to real company maturity. A durable target model is:

```text
Global navigation
↓
Company-defining hero
↓
Current technology / product evidence
↓
Selected products or platforms
↓
Technology / engineering perspective
↓
Developer or business pathway (only when real)
↓
Company/news/research pathway (only when real)
↓
Footer + legal attribution
```

The homepage is not a directory of every possible Jeclazon section.

### Hero
The hero should establish Jeclazon's company identity with one primary proposition, one concise supporting statement, and at most the actions justified by the current site.

Do not use fake customer counts, product counts, market counts, or unsupported leadership claims.

### Evidence
After the hero, demonstrate rather than repeat. Evidence can be an actual product, interface, technical system, published engineering work, documentation, research, or verified company development.

### Product discovery
When multiple products exist, introduce a scalable product discovery model. Each product should communicate:
- name
- concise purpose
- actual status where necessary
- audience/use case
- destination

Do not present conceptual products as launched products.

## Navigation maturity

### Establishment stage
Use only routes with meaningful content. A minimal company site may need only:
- Home
- Company
- Products/Technology if real material exists
- Legal

### Growth stage
Add routes when supported:
- Technology
- Products
- Developers
- Business
- Research
- Support

### Mature company stage
Potentially add:
- Careers
- Newsroom
- dedicated developer ecosystem
- richer product directory
- regional/localized experiences

Navigation depth is earned by content.

## Route authority

Potential long-term routes include:
```text
/
/company
/technology
/products
/research
/developers
/business
/careers
/newsroom
/support
/legal
```

This list is architectural capacity, not authorization to publish empty pages.

## CTA system

Use action labels that describe the destination:
- Explore [Product]
- View technology
- Read documentation
- Learn about Jeclazon
- Contact [specific function]

Avoid vague repetition such as “Learn more” everywhere when a specific label is available.

Primary and secondary CTA hierarchy must be visually consistent.

## Page composition

Prefer:
- strong editorial sections
- alternating density
- meaningful visual demonstrations
- clear transitions
- concise headings
- readable supporting text
- deep links for visitors who need detail

Avoid:
- endless card grids
- icon walls
- duplicated feature lists
- giant carousels
- empty statistics
- logo walls without legitimate relationships
- decorative dashboards representing systems that do not exist

## Audience pathways

Jeclazon may eventually serve public visitors, customers, developers, businesses, partners, media, candidates, and researchers. Do not expose a dedicated pathway until that audience has real material or actions.

## Legal placement

EJEP Systems attribution should appear where legally or operationally required. The public navigation and company storytelling should remain Jeclazon-first.

Legal pages must use verified legal wording and must not infer corporate relationships beyond what has formally been established.

## Product attribution

Independent products may use proportionate parent attribution such as a footer/company reference when appropriate. Do not force “Jeclazon” into the product name.

## Content acceptance test

Before publishing copy, verify:
1. Is it true now?
2. Can it be substantiated?
3. Does it distinguish current capability from ambition?
4. Is the reader told what matters before implementation detail?
5. Can unnecessary words be removed?
6. Is the next action clear?
7. Does the copy sound like Jeclazon rather than a reference company?
